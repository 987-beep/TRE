1. TECHNICAL AUDIT OF TERMINOLOGY USAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In Corrections 6, 7, and 8, documents frequently used terms such as:
- "Detached manifest signature"
- "Cryptographic signature"
- "Signature verification"
- "Manifest authentication"

CRITICAL TECHNICAL RECTIFICATION:
A `.sha256` text file containing a SHA-256 hex string and a filename (e.g. standard UNIX
`sha256sum` output) is a DETACHED CHECKSUM FILE, NOT A DIGITAL SIGNATURE.

Precise distinctions:
1. Detached SHA-256 Checksum:
   - Mechanism: A cryptographic digest computed over a file's byte stream.
   - Purpose: Verifies CHECKSUM CONSISTENCY and data integrity against transmission error,
     truncation, or accidental modification.
   - Limitation: Does NOT prove author identity, does NOT provide non-repudiation, and
     does NOT protect against an active adversary who can modify both the file and the
     checksum file simultaneously.
2. Digital Signature (Cryptographic):
   - Mechanism: Requires asymmetric public-key cryptography (e.g., RSA, ECDSA, Ed25519, GPG).
   - Purpose: Provides AUTHENTICITY, INTEGRITY, and NON-REPUDIATION backed by a trusted key.
   - Status in KAATYA Project: No digital signing keys or GPG mechanisms were used.

APPROVED TERMINOLOGY FOR KAATYA DOCUMENTATION:
- "Detached SHA-256 checksum file"
- "Manifest checksum comparison"
- "Checksum consistency verification"
Terms such as "digital signature" and "signature verification" are deprecated and prohibited.
