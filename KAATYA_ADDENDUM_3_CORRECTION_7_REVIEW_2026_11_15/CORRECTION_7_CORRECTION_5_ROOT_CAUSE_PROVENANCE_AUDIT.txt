3. TECHNICAL REFINEMENT: HASH STALENESS & EXTERNAL MANIFEST ACCOUNTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Correction 7 formally corrects the technical framing around the concept of self-
referential hashing:

1. Digest Staleness upon In-Band Modification:
   In cryptographic hashing, calculating the digest H(D) of a document D and then
   modifying D to include the text of H(D) creates an altered document D' = D + H(D).
   Under SHA-256's design (strict avalanche effect, pre-image resistance), H(D')
   will almost certainly not equal H(D). The embedded digest is thus stale upon creation.

2. Mathematical Nuance (Fixed-Point Feasibility):
   It is technically inaccurate to state that a self-referential hash is categorically
   impossible under all theoretical conditions. A fixed point x such that H(D + x) = x
   is mathematically possible in a finite domain. However, because SHA-256 is modeled
   as a random oracle with a 256-bit output space, discovering such a fixed point would
   require on the order of 2^256 hash evaluations, rendering it practically unattainable.

3. External Manifest Accounting as Practical Engineering:
   The standard and robust industry solution is External Manifest Accounting:
   - Primary evidence documents never attempt to declare their own post-serialization hashes.
   - All cryptographic digests are declared externally in a separate manifest (UPLOAD_MANIFEST.txt).
   - The manifest itself is authenticated externally via a detached signature (UPLOAD_MANIFEST.sha256)
     and by Git's content-addressable object store.
