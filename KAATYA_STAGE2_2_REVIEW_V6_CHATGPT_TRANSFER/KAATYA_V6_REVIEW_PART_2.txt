================================================================================
2. WINDOW GEOMETRY RECONCILIATION: FLOATING VS. TILED SNAPPING
================================================================================
A critical geometric conflict was identified between quarter-screen snapping and
the minimum window size on the 1366 x 768 reference baseline:
- Available Canvas Height: $768\text{px} - 48\text{px (Taskbar)} = 720\text{px}$.
- Quarter-Screen Tile Dimensions: $683\text{px (Width)} \times 360\text{px (Height)}$.
- Problem: If windows enforce a strict $640 \times 480$ minimum size, the $480\text{px}$
  height exceeds the $360\text{px}$ quarter-screen tile height!

Reconciled Dual-Mode Geometry Rule:
1. Floating Window Mode:
   - Minimum Window Geometry: $640 \times 480\text{px}$.
   - Free resizing, dragging, and cascading.
2. Tiled / Snapped Window Mode:
   - When snapped to a quarter-screen corner, the window manager overrides the floating
     minimum constraint, allowing the window to contract to $683 \times 360\text{px}$ (or $683 \times 336\text{px}$ with margins).
   - Responsive Behavior: Application headers contract to a compact $28\text{px}$ titlebar,
     toolbars collapse into overflow menus, and content areas engage internal vertical scrolling.
   - Half-screen snap geometry ($683 \times 720\text{px}$) remains well above the $480\text{px}$ minimum.
